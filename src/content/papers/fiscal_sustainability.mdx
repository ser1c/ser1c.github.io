## Overview

Many low- and middle-income countries seek to finance universal health coverage through
voluntary, premium-funded health insurance. This paper asks whether that model is fiscally
sustainable in settings where most workers are informal, enrolment is difficult to mandate, and
uptake remains limited. Using administrative data from Nepal's National Health Insurance Program,
we examine how membership growth, claims, and premium revenue have evolved since the scheme
launched in 2016, and how the resulting financing gap is transmitted to the facilities that deliver
care through lengthening reimbursement delays. We conclude that the binding constraint is not
enrolment or claims administration but the source of financing itself, and that durable coverage
points toward general-revenue financing rather than higher voluntary premiums.
